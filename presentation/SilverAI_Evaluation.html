The question

Market movement is a continuous, uncertain target

A single price rule cannot represent changing volatility, momentum, session context, or cross-market relationships.

SilverAI explores whether engineered market information can estimate the next 60-minute log return of XAG/USD. The output is an interpretable forecast and a possible directional rule, not a promise of profit.

Forecast
research objective

Estimate a future return, convert it to a price forecast, and expose uncertainty through conditional quantiles.

Instrument

Why silver against the US dollar?

XAG/USD is the spot price of one troy ounce of silver quoted in US dollars.

Silver is globally traded and responds to both precious-metal and industrial-market conditions. The project uses that market as a real-world test bed. The repository does not claim that silver is inherently more predictable than another instrument.

Model framing

Regression estimates magnitude. Classification assigns a category.

Classification

A classifier estimates a class probability such as UP or DOWN. The existing spot terminal also exposes a binary classifier path.

  • Category output
  • Confidence threshold
  • Directional interpretation

Regression

A regressor estimates a continuous forward return. SilverAI converts that return into a price forecast and quantile range.

  • Numerical return
  • Price conversion
  • Post-prediction threshold

The two ideas are related but they are not the same decision rule. The regression threshold is a post-prediction parameter, not a LightGBM training parameter.

Target

The model predicts relative movement in log-return units

r(t,t+12) = log(C(t+12) / C(t))

Log returns express movement relative to the current price. They reduce dependence on the absolute price level and can be added across consecutive periods.

The forecast returns to price through:

Predicted price = current price × exp(predicted return)

Design choice

Why LightGBM was a suitable engineering choice

Linear regression

Simple and interpretable, but limited when feature effects and interactions are nonlinear.

LightGBM

Gradient-boosted trees fit the engineered tabular representation and support Huber and quantile objectives.

Random forest

Many independent trees can be robust, but they do not provide the same sequential boosting objective.

XGBoost

Another strong gradient-boosting family. This project uses LightGBM as its selected implementation.

LSTM

Sequence-oriented neural architecture. It would require a different input and training design.

Transformers

Powerful sequence models, but a larger engineering and validation surface for this tabular study.

The repository does not contain a controlled head-to-head experiment proving LightGBM outperforms every alternative listed here.

Mean model

Huber loss limits the influence of extreme residuals

Lδ(e) = 0.5e² when |e| ≤ δ
Lδ(e) = δ(|e| − 0.5δ) when |e| > δ
MSE keeps growing quadraticallyHuber becomes linear for large errors

The project configures the mean model with the Huber objective. It retains quadratic sensitivity for small errors while reducing the leverage of unusually large residuals.

Uncertainty representation

Q10 to Q90 describes a conditional interval

A quantile answers a conditional distribution question. Q50 is the median estimate. Q10 and Q90 describe lower and upper locations in the predicted return distribution.

Interval width = Q90 − Q10
Interpretation limits

Quantiles are not guaranteed minimum and maximum values. They are not a calibrated confidence interval unless calibration has been demonstrated. Independently trained quantile models can cross.

Decision layer

A forecast becomes a signal only after a threshold rule

BUY

Predicted return ≥ positive threshold T.

SELL

Predicted return ≤ negative threshold −T.

NO CLEAR SIGNAL

Forecast remains inside the neutral band.

BUY if r̂ ≥ T    |    SELL if r̂ ≤ −T    |    otherwise wait

The rule is separate from model fitting. It converts a continuous estimate into a selective action label. A no-signal outcome is a valid result, especially when uncertainty and costs are material.

Source: reports/spot_v3_20261117_threshold_study/experiment_report.md, src/prediction/signals.py

Interpretation

A higher hit rate does not guarantee positive net return

Illustrative trade set
6 wins

+0.04% each

4 losses

−0.08% each

Hit rate: 60%. Gross return: negative. Costs make the outcome more negative.

Profitability depends on the size and distribution of returns, not only the share of correct directions.

Trade count, signal coverage, entry and exit costs, slippage, holding horizon, and fold consistency all change the interpretation.

Current evidence

The project has a measured research result, not a profitability claim

Verified findings

  • 34-feature regression contract confirmed.
  • Four LightGBM artifacts load from a separate regression report.
  • Development-fold mean sign accuracy averaged 49.29%.
  • Threshold grid produced no defensible selection.
  • Final holdout was excluded from threshold selection.

Limitations

  • Target-interval-aware purge correction remains pending.
  • Regression artifacts remain research artifacts.
  • Quantile calibration was not demonstrated.
  • Live provider freshness depends on an owner-supplied bridge.
  • No live profitability claim is supported.
